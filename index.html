How TruthLens works

The goal is to make the reasoning visible and the limitations obvious.

Step 1 — Extract

Identify specific, checkable claims. Opinions, predictions, jokes, and vague wording should be marked separately rather than assigned a false/true verdict.

Step 2 — Search existing checks

Search fact-check databases and trusted research collections for the same claim, including dates and context.

Step 3 — Find original evidence

Prefer the underlying dataset, court ruling, legislation, official transcript, research paper, or direct recording over a chain of articles repeating one another.

Step 4 — Compare

Look for independent corroboration and meaningful contradictory evidence. Record source date, author, methods, and limitations.

Step 5 — Explain the result

Use calibrated outcomes such as Supported, Contradicted, Misleading/Needs Context, Unverified, and Opinion/Not Checkable. Always show evidence and uncertainty.

Important technical limitation

This starter is a static front-end. A real automated service needs a secure backend for page extraction, speech transcription, claim matching, AI-assisted analysis, API calls, rate limiting, and source citations. Do not put private API keys in GitHub Pages JavaScript.
